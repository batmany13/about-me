## Interesting Tech

<!-- fnr:tech -->
- **Abliteration** — most open models refuse through what is effectively a single internal direction; find it and suppress it, and the refusals mostly go away at a small cost elsewhere ([Arditi et al., NeurIPS 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.11717), shown on 13 open chat models up to 72B).  It has gone from paper to commodity: [Heretic](https://github.com/p-e-w/heretic) automates it for free (~36K stars), and Hugging Face holds more than 1,000 community "abliterated" models.  Meanwhile the labs now vet security defenders and serve them directly.
- **Repository wikis and context files as agent context** — auto-generated wikis (DeepWiki, and open generators like [deepwiki-open](https://github.com/AsyncFuncAI/deepwiki-open)) promise agents a map of the codebase.  The [CodeWiki paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.24428) reports its generator beating a DeepWiki baseline 68.79% to 64.06% on its own benchmark, but a [controlled study of repository context files](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.11988) reports they don't generally improve coding-agent success and raise inference cost by more than 20%.  It matches what I measured this week: the material being researched was about 1% of what each worker was sent, and the rest was instructions.  More context isn't better context; measure what the agent actually uses before adding more.
- **[LLMs are making formal software verification feasible at a much larger scale](https://www.anthropic.com/research/formal-verification)** (Anthropic, Oct 22) — Claude wrote proofs, checked by Rocq, that the fuzz-tested entry points of 123 open-source libraries never crash on any input under 64 MB: 239 entry points, about 180 million lines of proof, and hundreds of bugs found along the way.  Testing shows bugs; a proof shows their absence, for what the spec covers.  The limits are stated plainly: single-threaded RISC-V binaries, proofs that can cost hundreds of millions of tokens, and "verified" still not meaning bug-free.  What stood out: the specs came from tests the projects already had, so the proofs build on existing work rather than new specifications.
- **[Recursive Flint](https://recursive.com/updates/introducing-recursive-flint)** (Oct 23) — an Apache 2.0 open-weight coding model, 40B total parameters with 2.5B active per token, running at 125+ tokens/s on an M4 Pro MacBook Pro.  It claims to lead mixture-of-experts models under 50B on agentic coding benchmarks and to rival 25–40B dense models at more than 5x the speed, though the post doesn't give the scores.  Built in under four months by a small team of people and agents.  What stood out: agents helped build nearly every part of the stack, from searching the architecture to writing the kernels.
