## Top of Mind for Founders

<!-- fnr:top_of_mind -->
There's pricing, sales motion, product goals, shipping features, but take a step back and think about the overall positioning of your product.  What is at the "core" of what you offer?  Is that value clear, and if you had to write a simple summary, can you list out the capabilities in a few bullet points?  It's not always about the fancy site, or all the features, but the vision you have for the company and product and how you are taking a step in realizing it.

## Interesting Tech

<!-- fnr:tech -->
- **[pi.dev](https://pi.dev)** — Earendil's minimalist coding-agent harness and SDK.  It embeds agent sessions in TypeScript with replaceable resources, models and tools, so you control the assembled harness instead of taking a vendor's defaults.  A separate, experimental Pi Durable adds persisted, recoverable tasks.
- **[Strands Agents](https://github.com/strands-agents/harness-sdk)** — AWS's open-source, model-driven agent loop for Python and TypeScript: composable tools, model adapters, sessions, and hooks for approvals and pause/resume.  You choose the tools, providers and persistence — and recovery, plus safe replay of anything with side effects, stays yours to own.
- **[Pydantic AI](https://ai.pydantic.dev)** — the Pydantic team's type-safe agent framework: typed dependencies, tools and validated outputs around an agent, with durable execution through integrations like Temporal and DBOS.  A schema-valid answer still isn't a correct one.

**Factories vs. harnesses:**  A harness runs one agent session well — the loop, the tools, the context window, the permissions.  A factory is a standing delivery process built around harnesses: a work item keeps its identity from triage through planning, implementation and review, with specialist stages, a coordinator, acceptance checks and a human decision at the end.  A factory can use several harnesses at once, one model implementing and another reviewing.  Several agents or a recurring timer don't make a factory; a retained lifecycle, stage-specific responsibilities and evidence that the work was accepted do.  One thing to check: "self-hosted" often means execution runs in your environment while coordination and run history stay with the vendor.
<!-- /fnr:tech -->
