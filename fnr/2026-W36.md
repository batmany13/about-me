## Building

_506 commits · 59 PRs · 2 open · ~72 commits/day_

Last week's `Next` said storage and APIs first, and that is where the week went.  The tech side moved its store from files into a database — a real data model, an API on top of it, and an agent-facing interface on top of that — with the site building identically from either source, and the two proved equivalent in both directions.  The part I didn't expect to matter: the whole thing now moves between hosting providers by a scripted procedure in about two seconds, with no files in the repo changing.  Authority stays with the files for now, deliberately, until a gated step says otherwise.

The research side got the check it was missing.  A subject evaluated once could only be evaluated again from scratch, throwing away what had been measured; it can now be re-checked weekly against what is already known, and the first run of that found two dozen measured results the ledger had been reading as never run.  The statements the system makes about *why* a technology matters now go through two cold judges before they stand, and the first pass caught three headlines claiming more than had been measured.

The direction work went further than I expected: the manifesto retitled and published, the two things I'm building given names, a new outcome opened, and a check that asks, while the work is happening, whether it still serves the thing I said I was doing.  By commit count it was a tenth of the week; by what it decided, a third.  The rest was the instruments the repo runs on — the weekly record rebuilt as two passes, the merge gate cut from six minutes to under ninety seconds by running once instead of a thousand times a week, one authored agent surface serving two runtimes — a quarter of the commits and none of the decisions.

**Corrections.**  Nineteen across both repos this week, and most share one shape: the record asserted something its own evidence could not see, or that had stopped being true.  A hosting choice recorded that nobody made.  A rule quoted from a branch that was never merged.  A customer quoted as live who wasn't.  A call recorded as held that had been moved.  None of these was false when written; each was a write that decayed, in a system that would never have re-read it.  The fix in every case was reading new evidence *against* what the file already said, and letting the file lose.  The smaller shape was an agent turning one inference into a rule — inventing a test from a premise I never held and flagging a dozen records with it.

**The trip.**  The fun half of the week happened in the family repo: a solo driving trip, planned the way I plan everything else.  The reframe that settled every argument after it was that this is a *driving* trip, not a sightseeing one.  Three routes explored, one kept, the other two filed as the bailout rather than deleted.  The unlock was collecting the car the night before, which is what turns a morning start from aspirational into real, and every clock got rebuilt on an early floor on the rule that what matters is daylight on the good stretches, not arriving before dark.  It settled as four days through the Southwest, a long day across the Navajo Nation chosen over a night in Santa Fe — written as a ceiling, not a schedule.

**Learning.**  <!-- fnr:building_learning -->
Building for real is hard, even with AI — so go back to basics.  Build it from the ground up: data model, contracts, APIs.  Separate the agent roles cleanly, and build them individually.

**A northstar, because direction is the scarce thing.**

One is raising, and the hard part is *who*, not *whether* — an investor who can hold the technical and the business conversation at once.  One is expanding from a product into an adjacent offering, and the question is which customer it is really for.  One is evolving the product for the third time, and the question is what the customer buys now that they didn't before.  And one isn't technical at all and is shipping more than teams five times the size, because the tools now let the person with the domain build the thing.

## Top of Mind for Founders

<!-- fnr:top_of_mind -->
If I think about a technical leader helping to guide founders, there are 3 major roles for a leader: setting vision, talent management (hiring, firing and promotion) and technical leadership.  This week was focused on hiring.  Whether it's the first engineer or the fifth, or hiring the first EM, this is your chance to scale.  No matter where you are in your journey, invest in the time: figure out your hiring policy; if you don't have career ladders, decide if it matters; and if you're going to hire your first people manager, what is their role, and how will this affect the makeup and demeanor of your existing team?

## On the Bench

- **Durable execution without a workflow engine** — a Postgres-owned recovery state machine (Absurd), and LangGraph read against Temporal as the comparator.  The question is where the exactly-once guarantee actually lives once the job table is the database, which is exactly the decision the build above just made.
- **Agent-evaluation harnesses and the sandbox under them** — the SWE-bench family (SWE-smith, SWE-Interact, Dialogue SWE-Bench), Harbor, and NVIDIA's NeMo Gym for post-training environments.  Every coding-agent claim cites one of these and the substrate that actually executes the run is the part nobody documents.
- **The employee AI workspace question** — the open multi-model Cowork forks and self-hosted workspaces (Open WebUI, LibreChat, Onyx) on open-weight models, the 2026 consolidation of the provider-router category (Portkey to Palo Alto, OpenRouter to Stripe), and whether "compile repeated usage into a deterministic workflow" is a layer or a feature.  I want to know where the good-enough line sits for non-coding work, because every routing benchmark I can find measures coding.
- **Repository-specific post-training as a ladder** — Bespoke Labs' Inkling write-up: context and tools first, executable repo tasks and graders next, fine-tuning only for stable repeated work, RL only where it adds measured value.  The most useful answer I've read to "when should a team stop prompting and start training."
