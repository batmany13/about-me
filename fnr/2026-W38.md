## Building

_194 commits · 15 PRs · 13 open · ~28 commits/day_

The migration closed.  Files to a database and a running service, finished end to end, and it landed on the Saturday — eleven days before the deadline I'd set.

Closing it meant splitting what was left across three new programmes: one for the product loop, one for operating the service reliably, one for making the agents better.  Two of the three didn't exist the week before.  The board now reads three active against seven complete, and nothing is sitting in a carry-forward pile with no name on it.

Meanwhile the running service met real production for the first time, and that turned up memory issues, processing gaps and caching needs — none of which would have surfaced in a test I could have written.  Each of them needed real traffic, and real website/agent interaction with the data store rather than the repo.

**The trip.**  Four days out of SFO, alone: US-50 the width of Nevada to Cedar City, then Kanab, Page, the Navajo Nation and Monument Valley on the way to Albuquerque, back along a stretch of Route 66 to the Mojave, and home over the Carrizo Plain.  I'd rewritten the plan two weeks earlier as a ceiling rather than a schedule — every "you can't absorb the miles" turned into a choice with a consequence — and in the event I didn't follow the routes exactly anyway.  Albuquerque was the stretch goal and I made it.  The thing I'd expected to be hard, booking beds ahead without giving up the freedom to turn around, came down to free cancellation and picking towns deep enough to have a room at 9pm.  Two days of the drive have no commits at all, which was the point.

**Learning.**  <!-- fnr:building_learning -->
Finishing is its own skill.  This was the first outcome I've closed against the manifesto and the bets — and I closed it by giving every leftover a named successor and one owner, rather than letting the unfinished quietly become part of what I'd just called done.  I got the split wrong first, carving it by technical layer, which produces pieces that can't finish independently; recut so that each outcome owns its whole result.
<!-- /fnr:building_learning -->

## Fund & Advisory

No founder conversations this week — it was a short one, half break and half coding.

One batch of learning is about how modern AI companies organize their eng and data teams, and meeting with various leaders and ICs to build this picture.  It's a standing thing now rather than a one-off — a small circle of operators I already know at companies worth learning from.  Two people added this week, neither spoken to yet.

**Learning.**  <!-- fnr:fund_learning -->
A company replicated a commercial product in a day — under three thousand training examples, and they published the recipe.  Four companies I've looked at in two months sell exactly that as a service.  So the arbitrage is real and weakly ownable: a data-design skill, not an infrastructure moat.  What survives is the data and the evaluation, never the training run.
